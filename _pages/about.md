## Background

### Brain-computer interfaces to electroencephalography
As a grad student of speech-motor systems theorist Frank Guenther, my first major project was to assist then-postdoc (and now-professor) Jon Brumberg in analyzing data from ER, a patient with locked-in syndrome who'd received an experimental electrode implanted in his speech motor cortex. Surgically-invasive brain computer interfaces like these are ultimately a last resort; after the project concluded, Frank, Jon and I all sought a BCI that could be developed and tested noninvasively. This meant we would need to rely on *electroencephalography* (or EEG): measurements of electrical potential taken from the scalp.
